## Design Context

### Users
Students, instructors, and mathematically curious readers exploring standard results taught from secondary school through graduate courses. They want to understand a result, compare genuinely different proof approaches, and follow a citation to the relevant source.

### Brand Personality
Editorial, scholarly, clear. The interface should support confidence and intellectual curiosity without feeling bureaucratic or cluttered.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a restrained scholarly editorial style: warm paper-like surfaces, strong readable serif headings, compact sans-serif controls, and a muted ink-and-amber accent palette. Keep the existing light theme. Approach cards should foreground mathematical content and use progressive disclosure for supporting details.

### Design Principles
- Put the theorem statement before proof approaches so readers know what each route proves.
- Keep the initial view calm: show approach names first, then reveal a sketch and source controls on demand.
- Keep citations concise and trustworthy; distinguish route-specific sources from general leads.
- Make icon controls keyboard accessible with explicit accessible names and visible focus states.
- Preserve readable mathematical notation and responsive layouts at narrow widths.
